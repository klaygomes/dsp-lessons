# The Echo With No Address

Objective: after this, you can find the delay of an unknown echo in a recording by taking its cepstrum (FFT, log of each bin's size, FFT again) and reading the quefrency of the tallest peak; you can explain why an echo D samples late ripples the gain every 8,000 ÷ D Hz, why the record needs more than 2D samples, and why a canceller working on a mixed recording must subtract its own shifted output.

Bug: calls through a partner network arrive with an echo (strength about 0.5) already mixed in, and its delay changes with the route. Lesson 2's canceller worked because it held the clean outgoing voice and knew the delay (2,000 samples); here there is no clean copy and no known delay, and the hardwired canceller adds a second ghost on a 1,200-sample echo.
Fix: act 2 of the lab: compute the cepstrum of an unknown call, read the delay off the peak's quefrency, guess the strength as twice the peak height (reads low), set the feedback canceller `clean[n] = heard[n] − strength × clean[n − D]`, and raise the strength until the cepstrum peak is gone.

Key words: per-sample step (the angle a dot moves per sample, 360° × tone ÷ 8,000; "turn" as a noun count stays a full revolution, as in lessons 3 to 15), lap (one full revolution, 360°), echo (not "reflection"; "ghost" only in the story), delay (not "lag", "offset"), strength (not "volume", "amplitude"; "gain" stays the lesson 3 word for how much the line scales a tone), ripple (not "wiggle", "wave"), log spectrum, cepstrum, quefrency (not "frequency"; "time" only as its unit), peak (for the echo's main one; "spikes" only for the row at D, 2D, 3D in the derivation), canceller, spectrum (lessons 12 and 14), wheel, bin, mirror (lesson 6's mirror arrow, and the mirror bin N − k).

Terms (as written in the prose):
- strength: how big the copy is compared with the original.
- ripple: the gain rises and falls in a regular pattern as you walk up through the tones.
- log spectrum: the logarithm of the size of every bin of the spectrum.
- cepstrum: the FFT of the log spectrum. It shows which periods the log spectrum repeats with: a peak at position D means an echo D samples late.
- quefrency: a position in the cepstrum, counted in samples or in seconds. A peak at quefrency 1,200 samples means something in the call repeats 1,200 samples, or 0.15 s, later.
- peak is used as the plain word, bolded once, not a new technical term.

Reused, with their earlier wording: tone as a wheel (360° × tone ÷ 8,000 per sample, the lesson 6 wording), gain (lesson 3), linear, time-invariant (lessons 1 and 3), unit circle and its mirror arrows, feedback, stable (lesson 6), DFT, bin, frequency resolution (lesson 12), FFT (lesson 13), window, leakage (lesson 14), the limit, alias (lesson 4).

Rules (the record rule is derived in the prose as bins per ripple = (8,000 ÷ D) ÷ (8,000 ÷ N) = N ÷ D > 2):
- An echo D samples late puts a ripple in the gain that repeats every 8,000 ÷ D Hz: D ripples round the whole circle of tones, so the cepstrum peak is at quefrency D.
- The record must hold more than 2D samples (N > 2D), which is the same as more than two bins per ripple, lesson 4's sampling rule applied to the ripple. A real voice wants several times that.

Misconception: "A long echo makes a long, slow ripple." · Why wrong: the ripple repeats every 8,000 ÷ D Hz, so a longer delay makes a tighter ripple (4 samples: 2,000 Hz; 40: 200 Hz; 1,200: 6.67 Hz; 2,000: 4 Hz), and its cepstrum peak moves further out.

Worked examples:
1. Tiny echo, D = 4, strength 0.5. 2,000 Hz comes out 1.5 times as tall from sample 4 on; 1,000 Hz comes out at 0.5 (the echo sits 180° behind). Gains: 0 Hz 1.5, 500 Hz 1.12, 1,000 Hz 0.5, 2,000 Hz 1.5, 3,000 Hz 0.5, 4,000 Hz 1.5. Log list at 16 bins 500 Hz apart (0 to 7,500 Hz, upper half mirroring the lower) repeats 0.41, 0.11, −0.69, 0.11. FFT ÷ 16 (the prose says so where 0.27 appears; a plain FFT gives 4.39): bin 4 ≈ 0.27, bin 12 ≈ 0.27 (mirror), bin 8 ≈ −0.13 (lopsided-ripple spike, doubled by crowding). Match at bin 4, the delay.
2. The ticket's call, D = 1,200 (0.15 s), strength 0.5. Ripple period 6.67 Hz, 600 peaks below the limit; zoomed table at 1,000 to 1,006.67 Hz repeats the four gains. N > 2D = 2,400. Pure echo, no voice: N = 1,024 puts the peak at 176 (1,200 − 1,024, wrapped), N = 2,048 at 848 (2,048 − 1,200, mirror; 1.7 bins per ripple). With the voice: N = 4,096 gives the right place but only 0.11, level with the voice's bumps (the window fades the record's ends, so fewer word-and-echo pairs count at full weight); N = 8,192 with a Hann window gives 0.20 at exactly 1,200, next peaks about 0.08 (multiples of the 64-sample pitch; the prose no longer lists which). Dip at 2,400: −0.04. Strength guess 2 × 0.20 = 0.40 against a true 0.5.

Derivation (book chapter 12, first edition, real cepstrum; one symbol D for the delay throughout, α for strength): ω tied to degrees (ω = 2π × tone ÷ 8,000; 1,000 Hz is π/4 = 45°; a lap is 2π) → y[n] = x[n] + α x[n − D], H = 1 + α e^{−jωD}, Y = X H → log|Y| = log|X| + log|H| → log(r e^{jθ}) = log r + jθ, so the real part of the log is the log of the size (checked: log(1 − 0.5j) = 0.112 − 0.464j, log 1.118 = 0.112) → log(1 + u) series with u = α e^{−jωD} → log|H| = α cos(ωD) − (α²/2) cos(2ωD) + (α³/3) cos(3ωD) − ... → spikes c[D] = α/2, c[2D] = −α²/4, c[3D] = α³/6, halves because a cosine is two arrows, the second showing as the mirror at N − D (bin 12 of the 16-bin list). The book's inverse-DFT note follows the derivation. Canceller: Clean(1 + αz^(−D)) = Heard, so 1 / (1 + αz^(−D)), poles at radius α^(1/D) = 0.99942 for α = 0.5, D = 1,200; raw-copy version gives 1 − α²z^(−2D).

Assumptions:
- Lesson 2's canceller predicted the echo from the clean outgoing voice it held and subtracted it; this lesson says so and introduces the feedback canceller as new, justified by lesson 6's feedback and stability.
- "Spectrum" means the size of every DFT bin, as lessons 12 and 14 used it; restated at first use.
- Lesson 12 taught the DFT as comparing a list with N wheels, and that a mirror-symmetric list has a mirror-symmetric DFT (bin N − k); the prose restates the mirror at the reveal.
- The lesson uses the real cepstrum (log of the size). The book's chapter 12 (first edition, 1989) centers on the complex cepstrum, which keeps the phase and can be inverted; finding a delay needs only the real one.
- The book defines the cepstrum with the inverse DFT; for a real, mirror-symmetric log spectrum the FFT ÷ N is identical, and the prose says why.
- History: Bogert, Healy and Tukey, 1963, coined "cepstrum" (spec reversed to ceps) and "quefrency" (a shuffle of frequency) while studying echoes in seismic data; from memory, not checked offline.
- The log(1 + u) series is stated, not derived, and checked numerically.
