# Storyboard: The Echo With No Address

**Problem:** calls through a partner network arrive with an echo (strength about 0.5) already mixed in, and its delay changes with the route. Lesson 2's canceller held the clean outgoing voice and knew the delay (2,000 samples); here there is no clean copy and no known delay, and the hardwired canceller adds a second ghost on a 1,200-sample echo. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 15. **Goal:** the viewer can say that an echo D samples late scales tones unevenly (4 samples, 0.5: 2,000 Hz × 1.5, 1,000 Hz × 0.5), because the echo arrow sits 4 per-sample steps behind (180° at 1,000 Hz, a full lap at 2,000 Hz); that this is a ripple repeating every 8,000 ÷ D Hz, so a longer delay makes a finer ripple; that the log turns the voice-times-ripple into voice-plus-ripple (swing 1.10 on the hump and in the valley); that the log list of the tiny echo repeats every 4 bins and its FFT ÷ 16 lights up bin 4, the delay, because the echo laps the tone D times once round the circle; that the cepstrum is the FFT of the log spectrum and its positions are quefrencies, counted in time; that the real call's cepstrum (8,192 samples, skip below 100) peaks at 1,200 samples, 0.15 s; and that the canceller subtracts a shifted copy of its own output, strength started at twice the peak (0.40) and raised until the peak is gone. **Emotion:** from "nobody will tell you the delay" to the surprise that a spectrum of a spectrum hands it over, then the quiet of the last bug fixed and a look back at the line.

Voice: the lesson 1 narrator, reused from its saved speaker (`01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt`, designed as `female, young adult, high pitch, american accent`), for series continuity. The file in this repo and the one in the original working folder are identical (same MD5); the run used the latter by absolute path. Exact command:

```bash
agent-voice script 16-cepstrum/4-video/vo.txt --out 16-cepstrum/4-video/vo --prompt 01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt --speed 1.1 --temperature 0.8
```

`--speed 1.1 --temperature 0.8` is the user's choice by ear. One non-verbal tag: `[surprise-oh]` at the reveal (before "And four is the delay", line 7); its audible "Oh" is captioned as "Oh,". No `[sigh]`: the series ends here, there is no next ticket. Tags never reach the screen. "cepstrum" and "quefrency" are voiced with a hard c through CMU phoneme spellings in `vo.txt` only (`[K EH1 P S T R AH0 M]`, `[K EH1 F R AH0 N S IY0]`, `[K EH1 F R AH0 N S IY0 Z]`); `lines.final.json` and the captions keep the written words.
