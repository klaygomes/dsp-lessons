# Storyboard: The Exchange That Moved

**Problem:** the old analog exchange is switched off and the new digital line must sound the same. A colleague's script runs the textbook recipe on the manual's filter (Butterworth low-pass, cutoff 3,400 Hz), and the copy passes 10.2% at 3,400 Hz where the exchange passes 70.7%; callers sound dull. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 10. **Goal:** the viewer can say that an analog filter has a response at every frequency from 0 to infinity; that the bilinear transform squeezes that whole ruler onto the half circle from 0 to 4,000 Hz once (no wrapping, so no aliasing), gently near 0 Hz and ever tighter toward 4,000 Hz (warping: 500 to 494, 3,400 to 2,363, infinity to 4,000); that it never changes a gain, it only moves it to a new tone; and that prewarping runs the curve backward, so a prototype designed at 10,607 Hz lands its 71% point on 3,400 Hz. **Emotion:** from "the recipe broke the sound" to the surprise that the fix is one number, three times higher than the line can carry, then the next ticket.
