In the basement sits the old analog exchange, and this month it gets switched off. Your boss wants the new digital line to sound exactly the same. The manual describes its line filter: a Butterworth low-pass, cutoff thirty-four hundred hertz, where it passes seventy-one percent. A colleague's script turns that into a digital filter, the textbook recipe.
So here's the mismatch. Analog frequencies live on a ruler that runs on forever. Digital tones live on half the wheel, from zero to four thousand hertz. Copying means deciding where each mark of the ruler lands. Wrap it round and round, and five thousand lands on three thousand. That's aliasing. Or bend it on once, and squeeze.
Run the squeeze backward. Which analog frequency lands on thirty-four hundred? Forty-four hundred? Five thousand? Start at thirty-four hundred on the digital side, go across to the curve, then down to the analog frequency. [surprise-oh] Ten thousand six hundred seven. More than three times higher, far above anything the line can carry. That's prewarping.
