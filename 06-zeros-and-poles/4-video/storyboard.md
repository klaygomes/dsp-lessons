# Storyboard: The Whine on Line Two

**Problem:** a cheap clock chip leaks a thin whine into every call, first at 4,000 Hz (the limit, inside the samples, where lesson 4's filter before the sampler cannot reach), then at 2,000 Hz after the chip is swapped. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 5. **Goal:** the viewer can say how a few lines of code remove one tone (every tone is a point on the unit circle; a zero on the whine's point removes it; how much a tone passes is its distances to the zeros, divided by its distances to the poles, compared with 0 Hz), why a real tone needs a zero pair (the mirror arrow), why zeros alone dull the voice (1,500 Hz keeps 38%), how feedback puts poles just inside the rim to make a notch (97%), and why every pole must stay inside the circle (stable). **Emotion:** from "averaging is too lazy to work" to the surprise that a pole beside the zero gives the voice back, then the unease of a ring that is left behind.

Voice: the lesson 1 narrator, reused from its saved speaker (`01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt`, designed as `female, young adult, high pitch, american accent`), for series continuity. Exact command:

```bash

```
