# The Bell After the Click

Objective: after this, you can predict a pole pair's ring (its pitch from the pole's angle, its decay time from the pole's radius), explain why a notch must ring, and choose a pole radius that trades the notch's bandwidth against its decay time to meet a spec.

Bug: every call connects with a relay click, and lesson 6's notch, y[n] = 0.905(x[n] + x[n−2]) − 0.81 y[n−2] with poles at radius 0.9, answers it with a faint 2,000 Hz "tink" that lasts about 5.5 ms; the whine switching on with the call rings the same way, and the tink is both at once.
Fix: act 2 of the lab: try to meet "ring under 1% within 4 ms" and "1,800 Hz at 90%" together, find it impossible (bandwidth × decay time stays near 1.46), then meet the accepted spec: ring under 1% within 4 ms, 1,500 Hz at 90% or more, whine at 0% (pole radius between 0.816 and 0.866; the line ships 0.85: ring under 1% in 3.5 ms, 1,500 Hz at 93.2%). The tink is not removed: it becomes too short to hear inside the relay click.

Key words: ring (not "echo" for this sound, except when quoting lesson 6's glossary line "how much an echo keeps per sample"; not "oscillation", "resonance" only once, for the spiral's response peak), the tink (the customers' sound), click (the impulse; not "pulse" or "spike"), decay time (not "ring time" or "settling time"), bandwidth (not "notch width" after the term is defined), spiral, pole radius (not "pole magnitude"), notch, passes / removes, zero, pole, unit circle, stable.

Terms:
- decay time: how long the ring takes to shrink to 1% of where it started.
- bandwidth: the width of the notch, in Hz, between the two tones on either side of it that the notch passes at 71%.

Reused from earlier lessons (wording kept): impulse, impulse response (lesson 2); "a tone is a dot turning 360° × tone ÷ 8,000 per sample", gain (lesson 3); filter, passes / removes, unit circle, zero, pole ("its radius is how much an echo keeps per sample"), notch, stable, system function H(z) (lesson 6); the 71% of the two-point average at 2,000 Hz (lesson 6).

New picture, built in its own section before use: the spiral, a wheel dot that turns by the pole's angle and shrinks by the pole's radius each sample.

Misconception: "A filter that removes 2,000 Hz can't produce 2,000 Hz, so the tink is a bug to patch." · Why wrong: the notch removes 2,000 Hz by subtracting a 2,000 Hz ring from everything that comes in, so a sudden input lets the ring be heard on its own; only its length can be chosen.

Worked examples:
1. One click through the notch: 0.905, 0, 0.172, 0, −0.139, 0, 0.113, 0, −0.091, 0, 0.074, 0, −0.060: the 2,000 Hz pattern (up, nothing, down, nothing), keeping 0.81 every two samples, so 0.9 per sample.
2. The whine switched on at n = 0 (1, 0, −1, 0, ...): from n = 2 on, x[n] + x[n−2] = 0, yet the output is 0.905, 0, −0.733, 0, 0.594, 0, −0.481, 0, 0.390, 0, −0.316: the whine leaks through and fades over the decay time.

Derivation (chapter 5, impulse response and frequency response of a second-order system): (1) after the input stops, y[n] = −r² y[n−2] is solved by A rⁿ cos(90° n); (2) partial fraction H(z) = 1.1173 − 0.2123 / (1 + 0.81 z⁻²), so h[n] = 1.1173 × click − 0.2123 × spiral, and at 2,000 Hz the fraction is 0.2123 / 0.19 = 1.117, cancelling the click; (3) decay time n = ln 100 / (−ln r) ≈ 4.6 / (1 − r); (4) near the top of the circle the zero-to-pole distance ratio is a / √((1 − r)² + a²), which is 1/√2 at a = 1 − r, so the bandwidth is about (1 − r) × 8,000 / π Hz; (5) the product is ≈ 4.6 / π ≈ 1.46, with no r and no sampling rate in it. In the prose, steps 3 to 5 become step three, step four and a closing multiply; radians are introduced at step four, before use; the small angle is a (not δ, so it cannot be read as the impulse); lesson 6's distance rule is restated.

- [[lab-act2]] act 2, fix: the same slider; a meter for decay time against a 4 ms line, gain at 1,800 Hz and 1,500 Hz against a 90% line, and the whine at 2,000 Hz (always 0%). The notch volume factor follows the radius: (1 + r²) / 2, which is 0.905 at r = 0.9.

Assumptions:
- Lesson 2 teaches the click as the impulse and the impulse response as "send one click, record what returns".
- The "4 ms and the ring blends into the relay click" threshold belongs to the story's listening panel, not to a psychoacoustic fact.
- Natural logarithms are used once, in the derivation, with a numeric check; the main text gets the 43.7 samples from "solving with logarithms" and from trial powers of 0.9.
- 71% is tied to half the sound energy (0.71² = 0.5) without the word "power" or decibels.
- Partial fraction expansion is named once as the book's term. The inverse z-transform and the region of convergence are not introduced.
- The book's chapter 5 treats the second-order resonator's impulse response and bandwidth in terms of the pole radius; the bandwidth × decay-time constant (ln 100 / π) is derived here for this lesson's definitions.
