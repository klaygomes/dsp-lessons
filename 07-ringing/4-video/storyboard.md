# Storyboard: The Bell After the Click

**Problem:** lesson 6's notch removes the 2,000 Hz whine, but every call now connects with a faint "tink", like a tiny bell, and the frequency meter shows one thin spike at exactly the tone the notch removes. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 6. **Goal:** the viewer can say that a click through the notch comes back as a click plus a ring at the poles' angle (90° per sample, 2,000 Hz) that keeps the pole radius (0.9) per sample; that a pole pair in time is a spiral (turn by the angle, shrink by the radius); what decay time (to 1%) and bandwidth (at 71%) are; that bandwidth × decay time stays near 1.46 because the notch is a click minus a ring; and how the radius is chosen to meet a spec (0.85: 3.5 ms, 1,500 Hz at 93%). **Emotion:** from "the filter has a bug" to the surprise that the ring is how the notch works, then the calm of choosing its length.

Voice: the lesson 1 narrator, reused from its saved speaker (`01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt`, designed as `female, young adult, high pitch, american accent`), for series continuity. Exact command:

```bash
agent-voice script 07-ringing/4-video/vo.txt --out 07-ringing/4-video/vo --prompt 01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt --speed 1.1 --temperature 0.8
```

`--speed 1.1 --temperature 0.8` is the user's choice by ear. Two non-verbal tags: `[surprise-oh]` at the reveal ("The ring is how the notch works", line 8) and `[sigh]` before the next ticket (line 10). No tag reaches the captions or the screen.

Visual code, the same marks as the page's figures (`2-figures/figures.mjs`, `lesson.css`): zeros as hollow ink circles on the rim, poles as ink crosses; samples as ink stems with filled dots, zero samples as small dots on the axis; sample 0 of the click response as a tall stem with a hollow square, "off the scale" (the click-ring figure); the ring's size as a dashed ink-soft envelope; the spiral as straight ink segments between the dot's stops (the spiral figure), shadows as dotted drops to the horizontal axis; pole radius 0.90 solid with circle marks, 0.95 dash-dot with square marks, 0.98 dotted with triangle marks (the decay-time, pole-gaps and bandwidth figures); the 71% line and other levels dotted ink-soft; bandwidth as an ink bracket with dotted drops from the 71% crossings; the ring in tones (the meter's spike and the spiral's peak) hatched coral with a coral edge (the meter and click-minus-ring figures); the click's flat line ink, turning soft when the peak is taken away; the trade-off curve solid ink with circle dots labeled by radius; the accepted window and passed specs in teal (input), the rejected 1,800 Hz request in coral. Labels in Geist Mono at 48 px or more, the customer's words in Instrument Serif at 72 px. Motion grammar: stems grow with a soft landing at sample pace, curves reveal left to right, the spiral dot steps 90° and 0.9 inward every 12 frames, the hatched ring shapes flicker like a live meter, the peak flips and hangs from the click's line before the notch draws in; held scenes drift (scale 1 to 1.03, the hero 1.04). Hard cuts between scenes. Every element sits above y = 900, clear of the caption band.

Hero frame: scene 8, the notch as a click minus a ring: the click's flat line lands on "flat", the hatched ring peak rises at 2,000 Hz on "ring", flips and hangs from the flat line on "Take it away", and the notch draws in with "notch: click − ring = 0" on "out", just before the `[surprise-oh]`. The scene runs 91.80 to 107.50 s (67 to 78% of the film); the bite lands at 100.8 s (73%).
