The Phone Line93 terms

Glossary

Every term the course defines, in the words the lessons use, with the lesson that first explains it.

A

alias

A lower, false tone that gives the same samples as a higher, true one.

Lesson 4: Sampling and aliasing
all-pass

A filter that passes every tone at 100% and changes only its phase; each of its poles sits inside the unit circle with a zero on the same line from the center, at one over the pole's radius, outside.

Lesson 8: Phase and delay
analog filter

A filter that works on the smooth voltage itself, with no samples; it has a response at every frequency in Hz, from 0 to infinity.

Lesson 11: IIR design by the bilinear transform
analog prototype

The analog filter you start from, the one whose response you want to copy into a digital filter.

Lesson 11: IIR design by the bilinear transform
analytic signal

A version of the signal made only of positive tones, with every mirror arrow removed.

Lesson 15: The Hilbert transform

B

band

A stretch of neighboring tones that share one boost, named by the tone at its middle.

Lesson 3: Frequency response
bandwidth

The width of the notch, in Hz, between the two tones on either side of it that the notch passes at 71%.

Lesson 7: How long a filter rings
bar

The match a bin must clear for its tone to count; a key counts only when exactly one row tone and exactly one column tone clear it.

Lesson 12: The DFT
bilinear transform

A substitution, s = 2 × 8,000 × (1 − z^−1)/(1 + z^−1), that turns an analog filter's formula into a digital filter's and squeezes all analog frequencies, from 0 to infinity, onto the digital tones from 0 to 4,000 Hz.

Lesson 11: IIR design by the bilinear transform
bin

A numbered slot; bin k holds the wheel that makes k turns in N samples, at the tone k × 8,000 ÷ N Hz, and the match the recording gets against it.

Lesson 12: The DFT
butterfly

Takes the odd result and turns it by one arrow of the test wheel, then adds the turned result to the even result for bin k and subtracts it for bin k + N ÷ 2; four multiplications.

Lesson 13: The FFT

C

cascade

Filters run one after another, each one's output feeding the next one's input.

Lesson 9: Finite precision
cepstrum

The FFT of the log spectrum; it shows which periods the log spectrum repeats with, and a peak at position D means an echo D samples late.

Lesson 16: The cepstrum
clipping

Flattening anything outside the range, −1 to +1, to the edge.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
coefficient

A fixed number in a filter's rule that a sample is multiplied by.

Lesson 9: Finite precision
convolution

Building the output as a sum of shifted copies of the impulse response, each scaled by one input sample, written x * h.

Lesson 2: Impulse response and convolution
cutoff

The tone where a filter switches from keeping to removing; lesson 10 measures it, for its filters, where the gain is half, and lesson 11, for the exchange's kind of filter, where it passes 71%.

Lesson 4: Sampling and aliasing

D

decay time

How long the ring takes to shrink to 1% of where it started.

Lesson 7: How long a filter rings
decibels (dB)

20 × log10 of a gain; 10% is −20 dB, 1% is −40 dB, 0.1% is −60 dB.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
DFT (discrete Fourier transform)

The list of the matches of all N bins, one match per bin.

Lesson 12: The DFT
downsample

To downsample by 2 is to keep every other sample and drop the rest, so 16,000 samples per second become 8,000.

Lesson 5: Changing the sampling rate
DTMF (dual-tone multi-frequency)

The keypad's two-tone scheme, where a key plays the tone of its row and the tone of its column together.

Lesson 12: The DFT

E

even part / odd part

The turned even samples added up once, and the turned odd samples added up once.

Lesson 13: The FFT

F

feedback

A filter also looking at its own past output.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
FFT (fast Fourier transform)

The DFT split into even and odd samples again and again until only butterflies are left; (N ÷ 2) × log₂ N butterflies.

Lesson 13: The FFT
filter

A rule that builds each output sample from input samples, and with feedback also from its own earlier outputs.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
FIR filter

A filter with no feedback, so its impulse response is its coefficient list, and then it stops (finite impulse response)

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
frequency resolution

The spacing between neighboring bins, 8,000 ÷ N Hz.

Lesson 12: The DFT
frequency response

For every tone, how much the system scales it and how far it delays it.

Lesson 3: Frequency response

G

gain

How much a system scales one tone: output size divided by input size.

Lesson 3: Frequency response
Gibbs phenomenon

A cut-short sum of wiggles overshoots a cliff by about 9% of its height, no matter how many wiggles you keep.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
Goertzel

A filter with a pole pair on the unit circle at one bin's point, run for N samples, whose last two outputs give that one bin.

Lesson 13: The FFT
group delay

How many samples late a burst made of tones near one tone arrives; it equals how steeply the phase climbs there, the phase difference between two very close tones divided by their turn difference.

Lesson 8: Phase and delay

H

Hilbert transformer

An all-pass filter that turns every positive tone back a quarter turn and every negative tone forward a quarter turn.

Lesson 15: The Hilbert transform

I

ideal low-pass

A low-pass with gain exactly 1 for every tone below the cutoff and exactly 0 for every tone above it.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
image

A copy of a tone that upsampling creates at the mirror pitch, 8,000 Hz minus the tone.

Lesson 5: Changing the sampling rate
impulse

A signal that is 1 at n = 0 and 0 everywhere else, written δ[n]: a single click.

Lesson 2: Impulse response and convolution
impulse response

What a system gives back when the input is the impulse, written h[n].

Lesson 2: Impulse response and convolution

J

j

A number that means "a quarter turn counter-clockwise", so j × j = −1; the point at angle θ is cos θ + j sin θ, written e^{jθ}.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles

L

layer

All the butterflies that combine small DFTs of one size into DFTs twice that size.

Lesson 13: The FFT
leakage

The part of a tone that shows up in bins beyond the one or two nearest it; it happens because the block ends partway through a turn.

Lesson 14: Spectrum analysis with windows
left-right shadow

The dot's position as seen by a lamp straight above the wheel; its height, seen on a wall beside the wheel, is its up-down shadow.

Lesson 12: The DFT
the limit

Half the sampling rate, here 4,000 Hz; tones safely below it are stored truthfully, and the limit itself is a knife edge.

Lesson 4: Sampling and aliasing
linear

Doubling the input doubles the output, and two inputs added give their outputs added; it has to hold for every input, sums included.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
linear phase

A phase that grows in proportion to the tone, which is the same as delaying every tone by the same number of samples.

Lesson 8: Phase and delay
log spectrum

The logarithm of the size of every bin of the spectrum.

Lesson 16: The cepstrum
low-pass

A filter that passes low tones and removes high ones.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows

M

main lobe

The hump of matches around a tone, out to the first zero on each side.

Lesson 14: Spectrum analysis with windows
match

How strongly the recording agrees with the test tone, whatever the phase: the length of the walk of samples turned by the test wheel.

Lesson 12: The DFT
minimum phase

A filter whose poles and zeros all sit inside the unit circle; of all the filters that give every tone the same gain, it delays the least.

Lesson 8: Phase and delay
mirror arrow

A real tone is two half-size arrows turning opposite ways, and the second one, turning clockwise on the bottom half of the circle, is its mirror arrow.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
multiplication

One number times another, the one cost unit the chip cares about; a sample turned into an arrow costs two, an arrow turned by an arrow costs four.

Lesson 13: The FFT

N

negative tone

A dot turning clockwise; −1,000 Hz turns −45° per sample, and its shadow sounds exactly like +1,000 Hz.

Lesson 15: The Hilbert transform
neighbor rule

Add a tone's sample before and sample after, and you always get the middle sample times a fixed number, 2 × cos of the tone's step (1.414 for 1,000 Hz)

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
notch

A filter with one narrow gap in an otherwise flat response.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles

P

passes / removes

How much of a tone a filter passes is its gain, as a percentage, and a tone that gets out at 0% is removed.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
phase

How many degrees the output's dot trails the input's.

Lesson 3: Frequency response
pitch

What your ear hears of a tone's rate, counted in hertz (Hz), repeats per second: the faster the repeat, the higher the sound.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
pole

A point where the bottom of H(z) is zero, so near it the response shoots up; comes from feedback; its radius is how much an echo keeps per sample.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
position

One of the N places the numbers sit in between layers.

Lesson 13: The FFT
prewarping

Choosing the prototype's cutoff by running the warping formula backward from the digital tone you want.

Lesson 11: IIR design by the bilinear transform

Q

quantization

Rounding a number to the nearest value the chip can store.

Lesson 9: Finite precision
quefrency

A position in the cepstrum, counted in samples or in seconds.

Lesson 16: The cepstrum

R

radians

A turn measured by the walk along the rim of a radius-1 wheel; a full turn is 2π, so ω = 2π × tone ÷ 8,000 and π means 4,000 Hz.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
ripple

The gain rises and falls in a regular pattern as you walk up through the tones.

Lesson 16: The cepstrum

S

sample

One measurement of the sound at one moment.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
sampling rate

The number of samples in one second, here 8,000.

Lesson 4: Sampling and aliasing
second-order section

A filter that reaches back at most two samples on its input and its output, so it holds one pole pair and one zero pair.

Lesson 9: Finite precision
shadow

Where a line dropped from the dot lands on the horizontal axis, its left-right position; R cos θ for a dot at angle θ and distance R from the center.

Lesson 7: How long a filter rings
side lobe

One of the smaller humps on each side of a window response's tall central hump; lesson 14 restates it as a smaller hump beyond the main lobe, carrying leakage to bins far from the tone.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
signal

A list of numbers, one per moment; a call is a signal.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
single sideband

A shift that keeps only one of the two sidebands, the two copies a plain multiplication makes either side of each call tone.

Lesson 15: The Hilbert transform
stable

A filter whose poles all sit strictly inside the unit circle: its echoes always die out.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
stores

What the phone keeps after sampling, the list of samples; nothing else survives sampling.

Lesson 4: Sampling and aliasing
strength

How big the copy is compared with the original.

Lesson 16: The cepstrum
system

A rule that takes in one signal and gives back another.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
system function H(z)

The number a tone z^n is multiplied by when it passes through the filter.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles

T

test tone

Your own copy of the tone you're looking for, multiplied with the recording sample by sample and summed.

Lesson 12: The DFT
test wheel

A wheel turning backward, clockwise, at the test tone's speed.

Lesson 12: The DFT
time-invariant

Delaying the input only delays the output; the rule works the same at every moment.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
tone

A steady wave that repeats at a fixed rate.

Lesson 1: Signals and systems
tone as a wheel

A dot turning counter-clockwise 360° × tone ÷ 8,000 per sample, whose left-right position is the sample; 45° for every 1,000 Hz.

Lesson 3: Frequency response
transition band

The stretch of tones between the last tone a filter passes within 1% and the first tone it removes to below 1%.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
the true tone

The sound that went into the phone, before sampling.

Lesson 4: Sampling and aliasing
truncation

Keeping only part of a filter's coefficient list and setting the rest to zero.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows

U

unit circle

A wheel of radius 1 with every tone on it at once, each at the spot its dot reaches after one sample: 0 Hz at the right, 2,000 Hz at the top, 4,000 Hz at the left; the bottom half holds every tone's mirror arrow.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
upsample

To upsample by 2 is to put one zero between every two samples, so 8,000 samples per second become 16,000.

Lesson 5: Changing the sampling rate

V

voice band

0 to 3,400 Hz, what a phone line promises to carry; 3,400 to 4,000 Hz is a margin left empty.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows
volume factor

The number in front of a filter's input side, chosen so that 0 Hz comes out at exactly 100%.

Lesson 9: Finite precision

W

warping

The uneven squeeze of analog frequencies onto digital tones, gentle at the bottom and ever tighter toward 4,000 Hz.

Lesson 11: IIR design by the bilinear transform
window

The list of weights you multiply the ideal coefficients by to keep a finite piece of them.

Lesson 10: FIR design by windows

Z

zero

A point where the filter's output is zero; a tone on a zero is removed.

Lesson 6: Zeros and poles
zero padding

Adding zeros to the end of a block before the DFT, which puts its bins closer together.
