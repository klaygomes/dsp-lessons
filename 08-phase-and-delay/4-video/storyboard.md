# Storyboard: The Fax That Smears

**Problem:** a business customer's fax modem fails on the line. Its tones (1,000 to 1,800 Hz) all get out loud enough (98.7% down to 70.1%), but its bursts arrive shuffled in time: the 1,800 Hz bursts about 3 samples late, sliding into the next burst, while the fax allows 1.5 samples. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 7. **Goal:** the viewer can say that a filter scales each tone (gain) and sets its dot back (phase); that a steady wave is late by phase ÷ turn, and a pure delay has a phase that grows in proportion to the tone (linear phase); that a burst is many neighboring tones, so its shape is late by how steeply the phase climbs (the group delay: 11.6° ÷ 4.5° = 2.58 samples for 1,700 + 1,800 Hz; 0.31 to 3.24 samples across the band); that an all-pass (pole 0.65 at 1,200 Hz, zero at 1 ÷ 0.65 = 1.54 on the same line) passes every tone at 100% and holds back the tones near its pole, so the line's delays even out to 4.72 to 5.67 samples at the price of making everyone wait about 5 samples; and why the late tones cannot be sped up (the inverse puts a pole outside the circle). **Emotion:** from "every tone is loud enough, so what is wrong?" to the surprise that the burst's shape is far later than its wiggle, then the calm of a fix that only delays.

Voice: the lesson 1 narrator, reused from its saved speaker (`01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt`, designed as `female, young adult, high pitch, american accent`), for series continuity. Exact command:
