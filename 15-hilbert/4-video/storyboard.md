# Storyboard: The Voice Underneath

**Problem:** a newspaper's tip line disguises every caller by multiplying the call by a 300 Hz tone. Every caller comes with a second voice, just as loud, a little lower: a 1,000 Hz tone comes out as half 1,300 Hz plus half 700 Hz, and the ghost band (0 to 3,100 Hz) lies across the wanted copy (600 to 3,700 Hz), so no filter can remove it. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 14. **Goal:** the viewer can say that a real tone is two half-size arrows, the top one counter-clockwise and its mirror (a negative tone) clockwise; that the shift adds 13.5° per sample to every arrow, so the top arrow speeds up to 1,300 Hz while the mirror slows to −700 Hz, whose shadow is the ghost at 700 Hz; that the analytic signal keeps only the top arrows; that the Hilbert transformer turns positive tones back a quarter turn and negative tones forward, so adding its output turned one more quarter turn doubles the top arrow and cancels the mirror; that shifting by one arrow and keeping the shadow is single sideband; and that a real Hilbert transformer is cut to 31 coefficients, windowed, 15 samples late, needs a matching delay on the plain call, and needs 35 coefficients to keep the worst ghost under 5%. **Emotion:** from "I only multiplied" to the surprise that a quarter turn makes the mirror vanish, then the next ticket.

Voice: the lesson 1 narrator, reused from its saved speaker (`01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt`, designed as `female, young adult, high pitch, american accent`), for series continuity. Exact command (run through `voice.sh`, which takes the shared voice lock before the call and releases it on exit):

```bash
agent-voice script 15-hilbert/4-video/vo.txt --out 15-hilbert/4-video/vo --prompt 01-signals-and-systems/4-video/vo/voice.pt --speed 1.1 --temperature 0.8
```

`--speed 1.1 --temperature 0.8` is the user's choice by ear. One non-verbal tag: `[surprise-oh]` at the reveal (before "Gone", line 8), heard by Whisper as "Oh," and captioned "Oh, gone.". The `[sigh]` before the next ticket was dropped (see Voice). One phoneme spelling, `[HH AA1 N]` for Hann (line 10).

Visual code, the same marks as the page's figures (`2-figures/figures.mjs`, `lesson.css`): a top arrow is a solid ink arrow with a filled head; a mirror arrow is a dotted ink arrow with an open head (lesson 6's mirror arrow); the shadow is a filled square on the horizontal axis; a sample is the two half arrows laid tip to tail, landing on the shadow (lessons 6 and 12); the ghost is coral (a hatched coral bar or band, a coral mirror arrow); the shift and the fix are teal (the 300 Hz tone and arrow, the delay box, the 35-coefficient curve, the Hann window); a reference (the call tone, the original arrows, the voice band) is dashed soft grey. Tones are turning wheels as in lesson 3, one sample every 12 frames. The coefficients are stems with the window as a dashed envelope, as in lesson 10. Labels in Geist Mono at 48 px or more, "Hilbert transformer", "analytic signal", "single sideband", "gone", "ghost gone" and the 41% readout at 80 px; one note in Instrument Serif at 64 px. Every label lands on the word that says it. Motion grammar: waves scroll like a live scope; arrows turn continuously at their per-sample rate (seek-safe steps of 2 frames); the split bars and bands slide apart; curves reveal left to right; held curves and wheels flicker in opacity only, never in height. Hard cuts between scenes. All text inside the title-safe area (96 px from the sides, 54 px from the top) and above y = 900, clear of the caption band.
