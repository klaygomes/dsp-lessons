Sampling and Aliasing

A phone measures sound 8,000 times per second. You whistle at 5,000 Hz. What does the phone store?

The rule

Below half the sampling rate, a tone is stored as it is. Above it, the tone folds back, like a mirror.

How it happens

The phone cannot store a wave. It stores a list of numbers.

  1. It measures at fixed momentsEach measurement is a sample. The phone takes one every 0.125 ms.
  2. It knows nothing in betweenMany waves pass through the same dots.
  3. It keeps the slowest wave that fitsBelow 4,000 Hz that is your tone. Above it, a slower wave fits too, and the phone stores that one. This false tone is an alias.

What people get wrong

"Too few samples make the sound blurry."

They do not. They make a different sound, and it looks as clean as a real one.

You cannot fix an alias after sampling, because the dots are identical. So the phone network removes everything above about 3,400 Hz before it samples. Your whistle disappears instead of changing.

Recap

  1. Sampling sees only dots8,000 per second on a phone.
  2. Half the sampling rate is the limit4,000 Hz on a phone. Below it, the dots describe your tone.
  3. Above the limit, the tone folds backSo filter high tones out before you sample.
