Why? Picture a dot on the rim of a wheel. The sample is how far left or right it sits. Those samples are the dot turning ninety degrees every sample. One thousand hertz turns forty-five degrees. Four thousand turns half a turn, so its samples flip. That's the tone as a wheel.
The route's output is the same dot, only smaller and behind. How much a system scales one tone is its gain. Here, point seven. How many degrees the output's dot trails the input's is its phase. Here, ninety.
Why does every tone come out as itself? The route adds three copies of the input: fresh, one sample old, two samples old. Draw each as an arrow scaled by its weight, and add them tip to tail. As the wheel turns, all three arrows turn together, so their sum turns at the same speed. No new tone can appear.
