# Which Key Did They Press?

Objective: after this, you can compute a DFT bin by hand as a tip-to-tail sum of turned samples, say which tone each bin stands for (k × 8,000 ÷ N Hz), choose a block length N whose frequency resolution gives every keypad tone its own bin with an empty bin between neighbors, and decode a DTMF key from the one row bin and one column bin that clear the bar.

Bug: the support line must tell which keypad key a customer pressed, from samples alone.
Fix: act 2 of the lab: choose N so the eight keypad tones land on eight different bins with room between them (N = 205 recommended), then read the row bin and column bin that clear the 25% bar for each key press and decode a four-digit account number.

Key words: tone (not "frequency" for the sound itself; "frequency resolution" is the term), test tone (the two shadow tests), test wheel (not "reference", "basis", "kernel"), turn back / backward = clockwise (not "rotate", "demodulate"), match (not "correlation", "energy", "power"; "score" only as the verb in prose), bin, block (N samples), DFT, frequency resolution (not "bin width"), row tone / column tone, left-right shadow / up-down shadow (the dot's position seen from a lamp above / a wall beside), pile up / curl away, spill (the one-word stand-in for leakage), multiplication (the cost unit in the cliffhanger; not "turn", which means a rotation in this lesson).

Terms:
- DTMF (dual-tone multi-frequency): the keypad's two-tone scheme, where "a key plays the tone of its row and the tone of its column together" (prose: "This two-tone scheme is called DTMF").
- match: the length of the tip-to-tail walk of a block's samples, each turned by a test wheel; how strongly the recording agrees with that wheel, whatever the phase.
- bin: a numbered slot; bin k holds the wheel that makes k turns in N samples, at the tone k × 8,000 ÷ N Hz, and the match the recording gets against it.
- DFT: the list of the matches of all N bins, one match per bin.
- frequency resolution: the spacing between neighboring bins, 8,000 ÷ N Hz.
- bar: the match a bin must clear for its tone to count; a key counts only when exactly one row tone and exactly one column tone clear it (25% in this lesson's lab). Not in the series table's new terms; added for continuity, lesson 13 lowers it to 12% and lesson 14 depends on it.
- Helper words, defined in place, not glossary terms: test tone (a copy of the sought tone's left-right or up-down shadow, multiplied sample by sample with the recording and summed), test wheel (a wheel turning backward, clockwise, at the test tone's speed).

Reused terms (earlier lessons): sample, sampling rate, the limit, alias (lesson 4); the wheel (lesson 3, with lesson 6's canonical wording: 360° × tone ÷ 8,000 per sample, 45° per 1,000 Hz); phase (lesson 3); unit circle, mirror arrow, zero, pole, z-transform by name (lesson 6); the cheap 8-bit chip (lesson 9).

Misconception: "The DFT finds the tones in the recording." Why wrong: it only reports how well the recording matches N test wheels fixed in advance; a 770 Hz tone is reported as bin 20 (780.5 Hz) at 90% with spill into bins 19 and 21, and two tones in one bin cannot be told apart at any loudness.
Secondary pitfall, taught in "The tone that hid": a single cosine test misses a tone with the wrong phase; the match must be a length.

Worked examples:
1. Eight samples of 2,000 Hz (1, 0, −1, 0, ...). Cosine test at 2,000 Hz sums to 4; at 0, 1,000, 3,000, 4,000 Hz to 0. The same tone a quarter turn behind (0, 1, 0, −1, ...) scores 0 on the left-right test. The second test tone is the up-down shadow of the backward (clockwise) wheel, 0, −1, 0, 1. Sums (left-right, up-down): starts at the right (4, 0); starts at the bottom (0, −4); starts 45° below the right (2.83, −2.83). Length 4 in all three, and the arrow points where the dot started. The same sums walked as eight arrows against the 2,000 Hz test wheel (right, down, left, up, ...): 1 right, none, 1 right (flipped), none, ... = 4 right; for 0, 1, 0, −1, ...: four steps down = (0, −4). Full DFT: 4 in bins 2 and 6, 0 elsewhere; a tone at 0 Hz or 4,000 Hz scores N (8) instead.
2. Key 5 (770 + 1,336 Hz, two cosines of height 1 starting at phase 0), N = 205, eight bins. Matches: 13.1, 91.8, 11.9, 7.1 (rows at bins 18, 20, 22, 24) and 7.8, 93.9, 5.8, 3.0 (columns at bins 31, 34, 38, 42), i.e. 13%, 90%, 12%, 7%, 8%, 92%, 6%, 3% of N ÷ 2 = 102.5. Decodes row 770, column 1,336: key 5. The rule stretches: off-bin tones spill (bin 19 32%, bin 21 20%), and the empty-bin argument explains why the winner still wins.

The empty-bin argument (derived in "Tones that fall in the same bin"): a tone is at most half a bin from its nearest bin, which keeps at least about 63%; if a rival bin is at least two bins from that nearest bin, it is at least 1.5 bins from the tone and gets at most about 21%. Without the empty bin a rival can be half a bin away and tie.

Derivation (chapter 8), placed after "Your turn" and introduced as the place to slow down: W_N = e^{−j2π/N} with the bridge 360° = 2π (2π/8 = 45°); X[k] = Σ x[n] W_N^{kn}; one forward arrow on bin m gives e^{j2π(m−k)n/N}; same speed sums to N; different speed is a geometric series, shown by S − rS = 1 − r^N, with r^N = 1, so 0; a cosine is two half arrows, N ÷ 2 in bins m and N − m. Then X(z) = Σ x[n] z^{−n} is written out and z = e^{j2πk/N} gives X[k]: N evenly spaced points on the unit circle. The inverse DFT was cut (it drew a "no intuition" note and is not needed for the fix).

Assumptions:
- Lesson 3 teaches "phase" as where the tone's dot starts on the wheel; the series glossary has no lesson 3 entry yet, so this wording is mine.
- Lesson 6 teaches the mirror arrow (a cosine is the average of a forward arrow and its mirror), which explains why a cosine scores N ÷ 2 and why bin N − k mirrors bin k.
- The cost unit is the ordinary multiplication, so it never collides with "turn" (a rotation). Lesson 13 uses the same unit and the same chip: about 1,000,000 multiplications a second, a story fact, not a real part's specification.
- Lesson 6 builds H(z) for a tone z^n but never names the z-transform; this lesson names it (a signal's sum of x[n] z^-n) and writes X(z) out for the block.
- The 40 ms minimum key press is the commonly cited DTMF receiver figure, stated as "about".
- Spill is named "leakage" once and deferred to lesson 14. The empty-bin argument uses three spill numbers only; the shape of the spill (and why it is zero at whole-bin offsets) is lesson 14's.
- The fourth keypad column (A to D) is included because it is part of the standard and of the bin set.
