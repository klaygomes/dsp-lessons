# The Keys That Blur

Objective: after this, you can predict how a tone between bins spreads over a DFT, explain the spread with the two leftover chains (the tone's arrow and its mirror), and pick a window and a block length that tell the keypad's closest tones apart while still hearing a key ten times fainter.

Key words: block (N samples, lesson 12; not "recording", "frame"), match (lesson 12, a % of a full-strength on-bin tone; not "reading", "level", "magnitude"), test wheel, turn back, bin, bar (lesson 12; not "threshold"), thrown out (a press rejected by the bar rule; not "misread", "confused"), leakage (not "spill" after it is named; "spill" only as the callback to lesson 12), leftover arrow, chain (the tip-to-tail sum of one arrow's leftover copies), mirror arrow (lesson 6), main lobe, side lobe, window (only the taper w[n]), Hann window, Hamming window (lesson 10), zero padding, multiplications (the cost unit, lessons 12 and 13), loud customer / rural (faint) customer.

Terms (new, exact wording in the prose):
- leakage: the part of a tone that shows up in bins other than its own. It happens because the block ends partway through a turn.
- main lobe: the hump of matches around a tone, out to the first zero on each side.
- side lobe: a smaller hump beyond the main lobe, carrying leakage to bins far from the tone.
- zero padding: adding zeros to the end of a block before the DFT, which puts its bins closer together.
- Helper words, not glossary terms: leftover arrow (lesson 12's term, reused), chain (the turned arrows laid tip to tail), full pile (the straight chain of a tone on its own bin, the 100%; N ÷ 2 long for a real tone because each arrow is half-size), Hann window w[n] = 1/2 − 1/2 cos(2πn/N).

Reused terms and conventions: the wheel (lesson 3: a dot turning counter-clockwise, 360° × tone ÷ 8,000 per sample); mirror arrow (lesson 6, recapped with its own figure before use); ω in radians with π = 4,000 Hz and j as a quarter turn counter-clockwise, j × j = −1, e^{jωn} the dot (lesson 6, one-line recaps before the derivation); "central hump" and "side lobes" (lesson 10, informal there, named here); Hamming window, truncation (lesson 10); DTMF, DFT, bin, match, test wheel, block, bar, frequency resolution (lesson 12); FFT, butterfly, multiplications as the cost unit (lesson 13). The book is named in full at its first citation.

Misconception: "Zero padding improves frequency resolution." Why wrong: the zeros add no sound, so the main lobe width and the side lobes depend only on the real samples and the window; padding samples the same curve more finely (a 128-sample Hann block of 697 Hz still reads 38% at 770 Hz however far you pad; a padded 256-sample plain block of 770 Hz still reads 11.6% to 12.8% at 843.75 Hz).

Worked examples:
1. Napkin, N = 8, bins at 0, 1,000, 2,000, 3,000, 4,000 Hz, no window. 1,000 Hz (1.00, 0.71, 0.00, −0.71, −1.00, −0.71, 0.00, 0.71) matches 0, 100, 0, 0, 0%; every chain makes whole turns. 1,500 Hz (1.00, 0.38, −0.71, −0.92, 0.00, 0.92, 0.71, −0.38) matches 25, 60, 70, 30, 25%. Long chain against the 1,000 Hz bin: 22.5° per sample, 180°, 64%; mirror chain −112.5° per sample, −900°, 15%; 64 ± 15 gives 49% to 79% depending on the start, 60% from this start. 2,000 Hz bin: 64% and 13% (−1,260°), 70%. 3,000 Hz bin: 22.5% (540°) and 13% (−1,620°), 30%.
2. The keypad. N = 256, no window, row bins 22, 25, 27, 30. 770 Hz alone: 81% own, 13% in the 7's bin (long chain 12.2%, mirror 0.6%, so 11.6% to 12.8%), 10% in the 1's bin. A whole loud 4 (plus 1,209 Hz, which leaks 2.3% into bin 27): 8.9% to 14.6% in the 7's bin; thrown out in 204 of 256 start pairs (144 as 4-and-7, 60 as 1-and-4). Faint keys 7.7% to 10.2%. Hann at 256: worst leak 2.7%, weakest own 92%, faint floor 9.2%, bar 5% decodes all. The rule stretches: Hann at 128: a loud 1 matches 61% in the 4's bin; Hann main lobe two bins = 125 Hz against a 73 Hz neighbor. Plain 320 (longest that fits a press): leak 10.7% against faint 6.8%. N > 219 for Hann.

Derivation (chapter 10 of Oppenheim and Schafer, Discrete-Time Signal Processing, 1989): sum of e^{jΔn}; geometric series (1 − e^{jΔN}) / (1 − e^{jΔ}); identity 1 − e^{jx} = −2j e^{jx/2} sin(x/2), so |1 − e^{jx}| = 2|sin(x/2)| (the chord); match |sin(NΔ/2) / (N sin(Δ/2))|; zeros one bin apart; side lobes about 1 ÷ (π d): 22% near 1.5 bins, 3% at 10.5 bins. Napkin check 1 / (8 sin(π/16)) = 0.64. The mirror arrow is the same sum with −ω, named as the short chain.
