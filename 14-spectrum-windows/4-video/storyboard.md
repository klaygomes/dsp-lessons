# Storyboard: The Keys That Blur

**Problem:** the live keypad detector from lesson 13 (blocks of 256 samples, no window, a 12% bar, a key counts only when exactly one row tone and one column tone clear it) throws out loud presses of 4, because the 852 Hz bin (the 7's row, bin 27) clears the bar along with 770 Hz, and it never hears a faint rural line, whose keys score 7.7% to 10.2%. Lowering the bar to 6% hears the rural keys and throws out the loud ones instead. **Audience:** software engineers with no signal processing background who did lessons 1 to 13. **Goal:** the viewer can say that a tone between bins spreads into every bin (8 samples: 1,000 Hz scores 0, 100, 0, 0, 0%; 1,500 Hz scores 25, 60, 70, 30, 25%), and that this is leakage; that it happens because the block ends partway through a turn; why (lesson 12's test wheel turns each arrow back and lays them tip to tail; on the bin they pile up straight, off it the leftover arrow keeps turning, 22.5° a sample, half a turn, so the chain reaches 64%, and the mirror arrow's short chain nudges it to 60%; only whole turns close a chain); that the matches trace one curve, lesson 10's tall hump named the main lobe (zero one bin out) and side lobes (the first 22%, shrinking slowly); that the 7's bin sits on a side lobe of 770 Hz (13%), so a loud 4 leaks 8.9% to 14.6% there with the starts while a faint key scores as little as 7.7%, and no bar fits; that the Hann window (Hamming's cousin, zero at both ends) shrinks the end arrows, drops the first side lobe from 22% to 2.7%, and at 256 samples gives a worst leak of 2.7% against a weakest faint key of 9.2%, so a 5% bar decodes every press; that the window doubles the main lobe (at 128 samples a loud 1 reads 61% in the 4's bin); and that the length rule is where the bins land: with loud keys ten times the faint ones, every neighbouring tone needs about 1.75 bins of room (224 fails at 1.56, 208 works at 1.85, 256 gives 2.26). **Emotion:** from "the detector is broken in two opposite ways" through the surprise that one tone fills every bin, to the relief of a gap the bar finally fits in, then a calm rule about where bins land, and a cliffhanger.
